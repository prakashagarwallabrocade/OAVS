History

Q.112

Geography

Q.121
Which planet rotates from east to west (retrograde rotation), so that the Sun rises in the west there?
  1. 1. Mars
  2. 2. Venus
  3. 3. Jupiter
  4. 4. Mercury
Answer: 2 — Venus (and Uranus) have retrograde rotation. A day on Venus is longer than its year.
Q.122
Place A is at 75° E and place B is at 45° W. When it is 12 noon at A, what is the local time at B?
  1. 1. 4:00 a.m.
  2. 2. 8:00 p.m.
  3. 3. 4:00 p.m.
  4. 4. 8:00 a.m.
Answer: 1 — The longitude difference is 75 + 45 = 120°, i.e. 120 × 4 = 480 minutes = 8 hours. B is west, so it is behind: 12 noon − 8 h = 4:00 a.m.
Q.123
Which continent is crossed by the Equator, the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn?
  1. 1. South America
  2. 2. Asia
  3. 3. Africa
  4. 4. Australia
Answer: 3 — Africa is the only continent crossed by all three. South America has the Equator and the Tropic of Capricorn but not the Tropic of Cancer.
Q.124
The upper continental crust, made mainly of silica and alumina, is called what?
  1. 1. SIMA
  2. 2. NIFE
  3. 3. Mantle
  4. 4. SIAL
Answer: 4 — SIAL = Si + Al (continental crust). SIMA = Si + Mg (oceanic and lower crust). NIFE = Ni + Fe (core).
Q.125
Who proposed the concept of sea-floor spreading in the early 1960s?
  1. 1. Harry Hess
  2. 2. Alfred Wegener
  3. 3. Arthur Holmes
  4. 4. W.J. Morgan
Answer: 1 — Harry Hess (1961–62) proposed sea-floor spreading at mid-ocean ridges. Arthur Holmes had suggested convection currents in the 1930s.
Q.126
Which type of volcano is built by fluid, low-viscosity basaltic lava and has gentle slopes, like Mauna Loa in Hawaii?
  1. 1. Composite volcano
  2. 2. Cinder cone
  3. 3. Caldera
  4. 4. Shield volcano
Answer: 4 — Runny basaltic lava spreads far before solidifying, forming broad shield volcanoes.
Q.127
Which of the following is a block mountain, not a fold mountain?
  1. 1. Alps
  2. 2. Andes
  3. 3. Himalayas
  4. 4. Vosges
Answer: 4 — The Vosges and the Black Forest are block mountains on either side of the Rhine rift valley. The others are young fold mountains.
Q.128
Which of the following is an organically formed sedimentary rock?
  1. 1. Coal
  2. 2. Sandstone
  3. 3. Rock salt
  4. 4. Basalt
Answer: 1 — Coal forms from plant remains (organic). Sandstone is mechanically formed, rock salt chemically formed, and basalt is igneous.
Q.129
A fan-shaped deposit formed where a mountain stream suddenly enters a plain is called what?
  1. 1. Alluvial fan
  2. 2. Delta
  3. 3. Levee
  4. 4. Meander
Answer: 1 — A sudden drop in gradient makes the stream deposit its load as an alluvial fan at the foot of the slope.
Q.130
Which of the following is a depositional landform made by wind?
  1. 1. Mushroom rock
  2. 2. Loess
  3. 3. Inselberg
  4. 4. Yardang
Answer: 2 — Loess is fine wind-blown dust deposited over large areas (e.g. north China). The others are erosional features.
Q.131
Where is the tropopause (the upper limit of the troposphere) highest?
  1. 1. Over the poles (about 8 km)
  2. 2. Over the Equator (about 18 km)
  3. 3. Over the mid-latitudes (about 13 km)
  4. 4. It is the same height everywhere
Answer: 2 — Strong convection at the Equator pushes the tropopause up to about 18 km. At the poles it is only about 8 km.
Q.132
Consider the following statements.
1. The Earth receives energy as short-wave radiation and radiates it back as long-wave radiation.
2. The atmosphere is heated mainly by direct absorption of incoming solar radiation.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 1 — Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the atmosphere is heated mainly indirectly, by terrestrial (long-wave) radiation from the Earth's surface.
Q.133
Mumbai receives very heavy rainfall during the south-west monsoon, but Pune receives much less. Why?
  1. 1. Pune lies on the leeward (rain-shadow) side of the Western Ghats
  2. 2. Pune is at a lower altitude
  3. 3. Pune lies on the windward side
  4. 4. The monsoon does not reach Pune
Answer: 1 — Moist winds rise and shed rain on the windward slopes. Descending winds on the leeward side are dry: orographic rainfall and rain shadow.
Q.134
The Montreal Protocol (1987) is an international agreement concerned with what?
  1. 1. Reducing carbon dioxide emissions
  2. 2. Conserving wetlands
  3. 3. Phasing out ozone-depleting substances
  4. 4. Protecting migratory birds
Answer: 3 — It phases out CFCs and other ozone-depleting substances. The Kyoto Protocol dealt with greenhouse gases, and the Ramsar Convention with wetlands.
Q.135
Which of the following is a cold ocean current?
  1. 1. Gulf Stream
  2. 2. Kuroshio Current
  3. 3. Canaries Current
  4. 4. Brazil Current
Answer: 3 — The Canaries Current flows south along north-west Africa. The others are warm currents.
Q.136
Which of the following seas has very high salinity because of high evaporation and little freshwater inflow?
  1. 1. Red Sea
  2. 2. Baltic Sea
  3. 3. Bay of Bengal
  4. 4. Black Sea
Answer: 1 — The Red Sea (around 40‰) is very saline. The Baltic, Black Sea and Bay of Bengal are diluted by large river inflows.
Q.137
Showing different height zones on a map with graded colours (green for lowlands, yellow and brown for highlands, and so on) is called what?
  1. 1. Hachuring

  4. 4. Spot heights

Q.138
In a pond ecosystem, which of the following is a primary consumer?
  1. 1. Zooplankton
  2. 2. Phytoplankton
  3. 3. Large fish
  4. 4. Bacteria
Answer: 1 — Phytoplankton are producers, zooplankton eat them (primary consumers), fish are secondary or tertiary consumers, and bacteria are decomposers.

Economics

Q.146
Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during the year is called what?
  1. 1. Mixed cropping
  2. 2. Shifting cultivation
  3. 3. Crop rotation
  4. 4. Multiple cropping
Answer: 4 — Multiple cropping raises production from a fixed area. Mixed cropping is growing two crops together at the same time.
Q.147
Consider the following statements.
1. High-yielding variety (HYV) seeds need more water and chemical fertilisers.
2. The Green Revolution first spread mainly in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. Assured irrigation in these regions allowed HYV seeds, especially of wheat, to give high yields.
Q.148
Which of the following is NOT an economic activity in the national-income sense?
  1. 1. A farmer selling wheat in the mandi
  2. 2. A teacher teaching in a school
  3. 3. A homemaker cooking meals for her own family
  4. 4. A doctor treating patients in a clinic
Answer: 3 — Household work done for one's own family is not paid or sold in the market, so it is a non-market (non-economic) activity in national income accounting.
Q.149
The concept of 'social exclusion' in the analysis of poverty means what?
  1. 1. The poor are counted only by their income
  2. 2. The poor are forced to live in poor surroundings and are denied facilities and opportunities that others enjoy
  3. 3. Poverty is measured only in urban areas
  4. 4. The poor are excluded from the census
Answer: 2 — Poverty is seen not only as low income but as being excluded from social equality, benefits and opportunities, for example under the caste system.
Q.150
According to NCERT, which state reduced poverty notably through land reform measures?
  1. 1. Punjab
  2. 2. West Bengal
  3. 3. Kerala
  4. 4. Andhra Pradesh
Answer: 2 — West Bengal used land reforms. Punjab and Haryana relied on high agricultural growth, Kerala on human resource development, and Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu on public distribution of food grains.
Q.151
Which type of ration card is meant for the poorest of the poor households?
  1. 1. BPL card
  2. 2. APL card
  3. 3. Antyodaya card
  4. 4. Aadhaar card

Q.152
What is a major limitation of average (per capita) income as an indicator of development?
  1. 1. It cannot be calculated for large countries
  2. 2. It includes the value of free public services
  3. 3. It always overstates poverty
  4. 4. It hides the disparities in income distribution among people
Answer: 4 — Two countries with the same average income may have very different distributions. Averages conceal inequality.
Q.153
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).

Q.154

Q.158
What is the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)?

Q.160
Treasury bills issued by the Government of India are instruments of which market?

  2. 2. Money market

Q.165

Q.166
Odisha, like several other states, reserves what share of seats in Panchayati Raj institutions for women, more than the constitutional minimum?
  1. 1. 33%
  2. 2. 50%
  3. 3. 25%
  4. 4. 40%

Q.167
When one kind of social difference becomes more important than others and people start feeling they belong to different communities (for example, when caste and poverty coincide), the differences are called what?
  1. 1. Overlapping differences
  2. 2. Cross-cutting differences
  3. 3. Natural differences
  4. 4. Accidental differences
Answer: 1 — Overlapping differences reinforce each other and can create deep divisions. Cross-cutting differences are easier to accommodate.
Q.168

Q.169

  3. 3. Finance Commission
  4. 4. Inter-State Council
Answer: 1 — An independent Delimitation Commission, whose orders cannot be challenged in court, decides the boundaries and the reserved constituencies.
Q.173
About how long did the Constituent Assembly take to frame the Constitution of India?
  1. 1. About 1 year and 6 months
  2. 2. About 4 years
  3. 3. About 3 years and 6 months
  4. 4. About 2 years, 11 months and 18 days
Answer: 4 — From 9 December 1946 to 26 November 1949: 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.
Q.174
Which word in the Preamble means that the people have the supreme right to make decisions on internal as well as external matters, with no external power able to dictate to the government?
  1. 1. Secular
  2. 2. Republic
  3. 3. Sovereign
  4. 4. Democratic
Answer: 3 — Sovereign means India is free to decide its own internal and external affairs. Republic means the head of state is elected, not hereditary.
Q.175
Which Article prohibits the employment of children below 14 years of age in factories, mines and other hazardous work?
  1. 1. Article 23
  2. 2. Article 21A
  3. 3. Article 45
  4. 4. Article 24
Answer: 4 — Articles 23 and 24 form the Right against Exploitation. Article 23 bans trafficking and forced labour, and Article 24 bans child labour in hazardous work.
Q.176
The right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice is guaranteed by which Article?
  1. 1. Article 29
  2. 2. Article 28
  3. 3. Article 30
  4. 4. Article 26
Answer: 3 — Article 30 protects minority educational institutions. Article 29 protects the language, script and culture of any section of citizens.
Q.177
Which House of Parliament can, by a two-thirds resolution, authorise Parliament to make laws on a State List subject in the national interest?
  1. 1. Lok Sabha
  2. 2. Either House
  3. 3. Rajya Sabha
  4. 4. A joint sitting
Answer: 3 — Under Article 249, this special power belongs to the Rajya Sabha, as the house representing the States. It can also create new All-India Services (Article 312).
Q.178
What is the maximum gap allowed between two sessions of Parliament?
  1. 1. Three months
  2. 2. Six months
  3. 3. Nine months
  4. 4. One year
Answer: 2 — Article 85 requires Parliament to meet at least once every six months. In practice it has three sessions a year.
Q.179
Which Supreme Court judge is regarded as a pioneer of Public Interest Litigation (PIL) in India in the early 1980s?
  1. 1. Justice H.R. Khanna
  2. 2. Justice Y.V. Chandrachud
  3. 3. Justice P.N. Bhagwati
  4. 4. Justice M.C. Chagla
Answer: 3 — Justices P.N. Bhagwati and V.R. Krishna Iyer relaxed the rules of locus standi, so any public-spirited person could approach the courts for the poor.
Q.180
Under the Tenth Schedule, who decides questions of disqualification of a Member of Parliament on the ground of defection?
  1. 1. The President on the advice of the Election Commission
  2. 2. The Speaker or Chairman of the House concerned
  3. 3. The Supreme Court
  4. 4. The leader of the member's party
Answer: 2 — The presiding officer of the House decides, and the decision is subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan case, 1992).

Teaching Methodology

Q.181
The term 'Social Studies' gained currency through the 1916 report of a committee in which country?
  1. 1. United Kingdom
  2. 2. India
  3. 3. Germany
  4. 4. United States of America
Answer: 4 — The Committee on Social Studies of the (US) National Education Association recommended Social Studies as a school subject in 1916.
Q.182
Helping students understand how people depend on one another (for example, farmers, traders and transporters) mainly develops which value?
  1. 1. Aesthetic value
  2. 2. Disciplinary value
  3. 3. Social value
  4. 4. Vocational value
Answer: 3 — Understanding interdependence and cooperation in society is the social value of Social Studies.
Q.183
Which of the following objectives belongs to the affective domain?
  1. 1. The student lists the causes of the Revolt of 1857
  2. 2. The student appreciates the sacrifices of freedom fighters
  3. 3. The student draws a map of Odisha
  4. 4. The student explains the monsoon mechanism
Answer: 2 — Appreciation, attitudes, interests and values belong to the affective domain. Listing and explaining are cognitive, and drawing is psychomotor.
Q.184
Consider the following statements.
1. Aims are broad and long-term, while instructional objectives are specific and short-term.
2. Instructional objectives should be stated in terms of expected changes in learner behaviour.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. Aims guide the whole programme. Objectives are achievable in a lesson or unit and are written in behavioural terms.
Q.185
Which principle of curriculum construction says that the curriculum should include what will be useful to the child in present and future life?
  1. 1. Principle of conservatism
  2. 2. Principle of rigidity
  3. 3. Principle of uniformity
  4. 4. Principle of utility
Answer: 4 — The utility principle selects content that is of practical value for life.
Q.186
With whom is the idea of a 'spiral curriculum', where key ideas are revisited at higher levels of complexity, associated?
  1. 1. Jean Piaget
  2. 2. B.F. Skinner
  3. 3. Edward Thorndike
  4. 4. Jerome Bruner
Answer: 4 — Bruner, in 'The Process of Education' (1960), argued that any subject can be taught in some honest form to any child, and revisited in a spiral.
Q.187
Which method is most suitable for teaching map-reading skills such as finding grid references and using a scale?
  1. 1. Demonstration followed by practice
  2. 2. Lecture method
  3. 3. Story-telling method
  4. 4. Source method
Answer: 1 — Skills are best learnt by watching a demonstration and then practising under guidance.
Q.188
What is the correct sequence of steps in Kilpatrick's project method?
  1. 1. Planning → Purposing → Judging → Executing
  2. 2. Executing → Planning → Purposing → Judging
  3. 3. Purposing → Planning → Executing → Judging
  4. 4. Purposing → Executing → Planning → Judging
Answer: 3 — The project is purposed (chosen), planned, executed and finally judged (evaluated) by the learners.
Q.189
Which of the following is a merit of the deductive method of teaching?
  1. 1. It develops the habit of discovery
  2. 2. It moves from particular examples to a general rule
  3. 3. It makes learning completely activity-based
  4. 4. It saves time and suits the coverage of large content
Answer: 4 — Deduction starts from a rule and applies it, which is quick and efficient. Discovery and moving from examples to rules are features of the inductive method.
Q.190
Who developed brainstorming, a technique of generating many ideas freely without immediate criticism?
  1. 1. John Dewey
  2. 2. W.H. Kilpatrick
  3. 3. Edgar Dale
  4. 4. Alex F. Osborn
Answer: 4 — Alex Osborn popularised brainstorming in the 1940s–50s.
Q.191
In the supervised study technique, what do students do?
  1. 1. Study entirely at home without help
  2. 2. Listen to a lecture
  3. 3. Study and do assignments in class under the teacher's guidance
  4. 4. Watch a film in the auditorium
Answer: 3 — In supervised study, the teacher guides and helps individual learners while they work in class, which suits learners of different abilities.
Q.192
Arousing pupils' interest and linking the new lesson with their previous knowledge at the start of a lesson is which skill?
  1. 1. Skill of closure
  2. 2. Skill of reinforcement
  3. 3. Skill of blackboard writing
  4. 4. Skill of introducing a lesson (set induction)
Answer: 4 — Set induction prepares the learners mentally for the new lesson.
Q.193
The Herbartian approach to lesson planning is based on which psychological principle?
  1. 1. Operant conditioning
  2. 2. Apperception (new knowledge is learnt through its link with existing knowledge)
  3. 3. Insight learning
  4. 4. Programmed learning
Answer: 2 — Herbart's 'apperceptive mass' means that new ideas are understood through existing ideas, hence the step of preparation.
Q.194
Morrison's 'mastery formula' for unit teaching is which of the following?
  1. 1. Prepare, present, compare, generalise, apply
  2. 2. Engage, explore, explain, elaborate, evaluate
  3. 3. Plan, do, check, act
  4. 4. Pre-test, teach, test the result, adapt procedure, and teach and test again to the point of actual learning
Answer: 4 — Morrison insisted on mastery: teaching and testing are repeated until real learning occurs. The second option is the Herbartian sequence and the third is the 5E model.
Q.195
Which of the following is a non-projected aid?
  1. 1. Slide
  2. 2. Globe
  3. 3. Film strip
  4. 4. Overhead transparency
Answer: 2 — Globes, maps, charts and models are non-projected aids. Slides, film strips and transparencies need a projector.
Q.196
A test gives nearly the same scores when the same students take it again under similar conditions. Which quality does the test have?
  1. 1. Validity
  2. 2. Reliability
  3. 3. Usability
  4. 4. Comprehensiveness
Answer: 2 — Reliability is consistency of measurement. Validity is whether the test measures what it is meant to measure.
Q.197
Who proposed the model of 'mastery learning', in which most students can achieve mastery given enough time and corrective help?
  1. 1. Benjamin S. Bloom
  2. 2. Robert Mager
  3. 3. David Ausubel
  4. 4. Lev Vygotsky
Answer: 1 — Bloom (1968), building on John Carroll's model, used formative tests followed by corrective teaching.
Q.198
What is the main limitation of essay-type questions in an achievement test?
  1. 1. Subjectivity in scoring and limited coverage of content
  2. 2. They cannot test writing ability
  3. 3. They are easy to guess
  4. 4. They cannot test higher-order thinking
Answer: 1 — Essay answers can test organisation and higher-order thinking, but marks vary between examiners and fewer topics can be covered.
Q.199
The topographical sheets of the Survey of India commonly used in schools for map-reading exercises have which scale?
  1. 1. 1:1,000,000
  2. 2. 1:5,000,000
  3. 3. 1:50,000
  4. 4. 1:500
Answer: 3 — 1:50,000 (and 1:25,000) toposheets show relief, drainage and settlements in detail, and are standard for map-reading practice.
Q.200
A teacher wants students to compare the land use around Bhubaneswar in 2005 and 2025 using satellite imagery. Which tool is most suitable?
  1. 1. A wall map of India
  2. 2. A political globe
  3. 3. An epidiascope
  4. 4. Google Earth / GIS software with historical imagery
