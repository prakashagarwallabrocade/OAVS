History

Q.101
Samuel Crompton's spinning mule (1779) combined the features of which two earlier inventions?
  1. 1. Flying shuttle and power loom
  2. 2. Spinning Jenny and water frame
  3. 3. Water frame and power loom
  4. 4. Spinning Jenny and steam engine
Answer: 2 — The mule combined the moving carriage of the Jenny with the rollers of Arkwright's water frame, producing fine, strong yarn.
Q.102
The first passenger railway train in India (1853) ran between which two places?
  1. 1. Howrah and Hooghly
  2. 2. Bombay (Bori Bunder) and Thane
  3. 3. Madras and Arcot
  4. 4. Delhi and Agra
Answer: 2 — It ran 34 km from Bori Bunder to Thane on 16 April 1853, under Lord Dalhousie.
Q.103
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The Civil Disobedience Movement found strong support among peasants in 1930–31.
Reason (R): The Great Depression caused agricultural prices to collapse, while the colonial government refused to reduce revenue demands.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.
Answer: 1 — Falling prices and fixed revenue demands made peasants restive, and many joined the no-revenue and no-rent campaigns. R explains A.
Q.104
Consider the following statements about the Constitution of 1791 in France.
1. It made France a constitutional monarchy.
2. It gave all adult citizens the right to vote.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 1 — Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is wrong: only 'active citizens' (men over 25 who paid taxes equal to at least three days' wages) could vote. Women and poorer men were 'passive citizens'.
Q.105
What was the 'Great Fear' of July–August 1789?
  1. 1. A wave of peasant uprisings in which castles were attacked and records of manorial dues burnt
  2. 2. The Reign of Terror under Robespierre
  3. 3. The invasion of France by Austria and Prussia
  4. 4. The flight of the king to Varennes
Answer: 1 — Rumours of brigands spread panic in the countryside. Peasants attacked chateaux, burning documents of feudal dues, which hastened the abolition of feudal privileges on 4 August.
Q.106

Answer: 3 — Waterloo ended the Napoleonic era. Austerlitz (1805) was one of his greatest victories, and Trafalgar (1805) was a British naval victory.

Geography

Q.121
Which planet has the highest mean density in the solar system?
  1. 1. Jupiter
  2. 2. Saturn
  3. 3. Mercury
  4. 4. Earth
Answer: 4 — Earth (about 5.5 g/cm³) is the densest planet. Saturn is the least dense, less dense than water.
Q.122
On about which date is the Earth farthest from the Sun (aphelion)?
  1. 1. 3 January
  2. 2. 21 June
  3. 3. 23 September
  4. 4. 4 July
Answer: 4 — Aphelion is around 4 July and perihelion around 3 January. Seasons are caused by the tilt of the axis, not by distance.
Q.123
Why does the International Date Line zigzag instead of following the 180° meridian exactly?
  1. 1. Because of magnetic variations
  2. 2. To avoid dividing countries and island groups into different dates
  3. 3. Because of ocean currents
  4. 4. To follow the Equator
Answer: 2 — It bends around places such as Chukotka (Russia), the Aleutians and Kiribati, so that each region keeps a single calendar date.
Q.124
The core of the Earth is believed to be made mainly of which elements?
  1. 1. Silica and aluminium
  2. 2. Silica and magnesium
  3. 3. Carbon and oxygen
  4. 4. Nickel and iron
Answer: 4 — The core is called NIFE (Ni + Fe). Its outer part is liquid and its inner part solid.
Q.125
Which of the following is a minor plate, not one of the seven major lithospheric plates?
  1. 1. Pacific plate
  2. 2. Eurasian plate
  3. 3. Antarctic plate
  4. 4. Cocos plate
Answer: 4 — The major plates are Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian and Antarctic. Cocos, Nazca and Philippine are minor plates.
Q.126
The seismic 'shadow zone', where neither P- nor S-waves are recorded directly, lies at what angular distance from the epicentre?
  1. 1. Between 0° and 30°
  2. 2. Between 45° and 90°
  3. 3. Between about 105° and 145°
  4. 4. Beyond 180°
Answer: 3 — Seismographs between about 105° and 145° receive no direct waves. S-waves are absent beyond 105°, which shows the outer core is liquid.

  1. 1. Young fold mountain

  3. 3. Residual mountain formed by glaciers
  4. 4. Block mountain
Answer: 4 — The Satpura is a block mountain (horst) lying between the Narmada and Tapi rift valleys.

Q.129
Black soil is described as 'self-ploughing'. Why?
  1. 1. It is ploughed by earthworms only
  2. 2. It never needs irrigation
  3. 3. It is very sandy and loose
  4. 4. It develops deep cracks in the dry season, which aerate the soil
Answer: 4 — Clayey black soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry, developing wide cracks that help in natural aeration.
Q.130
An isolated residual hill rising abruptly from a desert plain, formed by long erosion, is called what?
  1. 1. Inselberg
  2. 2. Barchan
  3. 3. Loess
  4. 4. Playa
Answer: 1 — Inselbergs (e.g. Uluru in Australia) are erosional remnants. Barchans are dunes, loess is a deposit, and a playa is a dry lake bed.
Q.131
Long, winding ridges of sand and gravel deposited by meltwater streams flowing under a glacier are called what?
  1. 1. Drumlins
  2. 2. Cirques
  3. 3. Arêtes
  4. 4. Eskers
Answer: 4 — Eskers are glacio-fluvial deposits. Drumlins are smooth, oval hills of till, while cirques and arêtes are erosional.
Q.132
The 'Loo', the hot wind of north India in summer, is an example of heat transfer by which process?
  1. 1. Advection (horizontal movement of air)
  2. 2. Conduction
  3. 3. Convection (vertical movement)
  4. 4. Terrestrial radiation
Answer: 1 — NCERT cites the Loo as an example of advection. In middle latitudes, most day-to-day weather changes are due to advection.
Q.133
What is the Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)?
  1. 1. A low-pressure belt near the Equator where the trade winds of both hemispheres converge
  2. 2. A high-pressure belt at 30° latitude
  3. 3. A zone of westerly winds at 60° latitude
  4. 4. A cold front over the poles
Answer: 1 — The ITCZ shifts with the Sun. Its northward shift over India in summer (the monsoon trough) helps draw in the south-west monsoon.
Q.134

Q.135
The ports of Norway stay ice-free in winter despite their high latitude. Why?

  2. 2. Because of the warm North Atlantic Drift (extension of the Gulf Stream)

  4. 4. Because of the Humboldt Current

Q.136
What is the layer of ocean water in which temperature falls rapidly with depth called?
  1. 1. Halocline only
  2. 2. Mixed layer
  3. 3. Thermocline
  4. 4. Abyssal layer
Answer: 3 — Below the warm mixed surface layer lies the thermocline, about 500–1,000 m thick. Below it is cold, deep water.
Q.137

  3. 3. 1:5,000
  4. 4. 1:5,000,000

Q.138
Which ecological pyramid is always upright and can never be inverted?
  1. 1. Pyramid of numbers
  2. 2. Pyramid of biomass
  3. 3. Pyramid of biomass in an aquatic ecosystem
  4. 4. Pyramid of energy
Answer: 4 — Energy decreases at each successive trophic level. Pyramids of numbers (a tree ecosystem) and of biomass (oceans) can be inverted.
Q.139
Rivers such as the Indus, Sutlej and Brahmaputra, which are older than the Himalayas and cut gorges through them as the mountains rose, are called what?
  1. 1. Antecedent rivers
  2. 2. Consequent rivers
  3. 3. Subsequent rivers
  4. 4. Superimposed rivers
Answer: 1 — Antecedent rivers kept their courses by cutting down as the Himalayas were uplifted, forming deep gorges.
Q.140

Economics

  1. 1. Natural resources
  2. 2. Colonies

  2. 2. Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)
  3. 3. Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)
  4. 4. Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)
Answer: 3 — A nine-judge bench recognised privacy as intrinsic to life and liberty. Maneka Gandhi (1978) widened the meaning of 'procedure established by law'.
Q.171
Article 19 guarantees how many freedoms to citizens today?
  1. 1. Seven
  2. 2. Six
  3. 3. Five
  4. 4. Four
Answer: 2 — Speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession. The seventh, property, was deleted in 1978.
Q.172
Which Article of the Directive Principles directs the State to try to secure a Uniform Civil Code for citizens?
  1. 1. Article 44
  2. 2. Article 40
  3. 3. Article 45
  4. 4. Article 48
Answer: 1 — Article 40 deals with village panchayats, Article 45 with early childhood care, and Article 48 with agriculture and animal husbandry.
Q.173
What is the quorum needed to hold a sitting of either House of Parliament?
  1. 1. One-fourth of the total membership
  2. 2. One-third of the members present
  3. 3. One-tenth of the total membership
  4. 4. Half of the total membership
Answer: 3 — Under Article 100(3), the quorum is one-tenth of the total number of members of the House.
Q.174
Under which Article can the President issue ordinances when Parliament is not in session?
  1. 1. Article 72
  2. 2. Article 123
  3. 3. Article 352
  4. 4. Article 213
Answer: 2 — Article 123 is the President's ordinance power. Article 213 gives the same power to Governors, and Article 72 is the pardoning power.
Q.175
The President's power to grant pardons, reprieves and remissions of punishment is given under which Article?
  1. 1. Article 161
  2. 2. Article 72
  3. 3. Article 123
  4. 4. Article 143
Answer: 2 — Article 72 covers the President. Article 161 gives Governors a similar but narrower power. Only the President can pardon a death sentence.
Q.176
Under which Article can the President seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court on a question of law or fact?
  1. 1. Article 131
  2. 2. Article 32
  3. 3. Article 143
  4. 4. Article 226
Answer: 3 — Article 143 gives the Supreme Court its advisory jurisdiction, and its opinion is not binding on the President.
Q.177
Lok Adalats, which settle disputes through compromise and whose awards are final, get statutory status under which Act?

Q.178
In which present-day state did the Chipko movement of the 1970s, in which villagers hugged trees to prevent felling, begin?

Q.179
If there is a tie in voting on a matter in the Lok Sabha, what happens?

  4. 4. The Speaker exercises a casting vote

Q.180
President's Rule in a State under Article 356 is usually imposed on the basis of what?

  3. 3. A request by the Chief Minister of a neighbouring State
  4. 4. The Governor's report that the government of the State cannot be carried on according to the Constitution
Answer: 4 — The President acts on the Governor's report (or otherwise). After the S.R. Bommai case (1994), its use is subject to judicial review.

Teaching Methodology

Q.181
In the revised Bloom's taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001), which of the following is the correct set of knowledge dimensions?
  1. 1. Recall, recognition, reproduction and reasoning
  2. 2. Simple, compound, complex and abstract
  3. 3. Factual, conceptual, procedural and metacognitive
  4. 4. Concrete, semi-concrete, abstract and symbolic
Answer: 3 — The revision added a knowledge dimension (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive) alongside the cognitive process dimension.
Q.182
Which of the following verbs is a specification of the objective 'understanding' rather than 'knowledge'?
  1. 1. Compares
  2. 2. Recalls
  3. 3. Lists
  4. 4. Names
Answer: 1 — Understanding is shown by comparing, classifying, explaining and giving examples. Knowledge is shown by recalling, listing and naming.
Q.183
The report 'Learning Without Burden' (1993), which influenced child-centred curricula, was prepared by a committee chaired by whom?
  1. 1. Prof. Yash Pal
  2. 2. D.S. Kothari
  3. 3. Ram Murti
  4. 4. Kasturirangan
Answer: 1 — The Yash Pal Committee criticised heavy schoolbags and rote learning. K. Kasturirangan chaired the NEP 2020 drafting committee.
Q.184
Arranging subject matter according to the learner's interests, needs and mental level, rather than the internal logic of the discipline, is called what?
  1. 1. Logical organisation
  2. 2. Psychological organisation
  3. 3. Chronological organisation
  4. 4. Topical organisation
Answer: 2 — Psychological organisation is child-centred. Logical organisation follows the structure of the subject itself.
Q.185
In which method do students prepare and present papers on a topic, followed by a guided group discussion? It is most suitable for higher classes.
  1. 1. Seminar
  2. 2. Story-telling
  3. 3. Drill
  4. 4. Lecture
Answer: 1 — Seminars develop research, presentation and critical discussion skills in mature learners.
Q.186
Students collect data from households in their locality on migration and occupations using a questionnaire. Which method is this?
  1. 1. Survey method
  2. 2. Lecture method
  3. 3. Dramatisation
  4. 4. Programmed instruction
Answer: 1 — The survey method uses questionnaires or interviews to gather first-hand data from the community.
Q.187
The 'Zone of Proximal Development', the gap between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with help, was proposed by whom?
  1. 1. Jean Piaget
  2. 2. B.F. Skinner
  3. 3. Lev Vygotsky
  4. 4. Albert Bandura
Answer: 3 — Vygotsky's social constructivism stresses scaffolding by teachers and peers within the ZPD.
Q.188
In which cooperative learning technique does each member of a group master one part of a topic, teach it to others, and then everyone combines their knowledge?
  1. 1. Think-pair-share
  2. 2. Brainstorming
  3. 3. Jigsaw
  4. 4. Debate
Answer: 3 — Elliot Aronson's Jigsaw technique (1971) makes every student an 'expert' for one piece and promotes interdependence.
Q.189
A teacher first explains a single village's panchayat before moving on to the full three-tier Panchayati Raj structure. Which maxim is this?
  1. 1. From abstract to concrete
  2. 2. From simple to complex
  3. 3. From whole to part
  4. 4. From rational to empirical

Q.190
Allen and Ryan of Stanford University identified how many teaching skills for micro-teaching?
  1. 1. 8
  2. 2. 21
  3. 3. 14
  4. 4. 5
Answer: 3 — They listed 14 skills, including stimulus variation, set induction, closure, reinforcement, fluency in questioning and probing. Indian programmes often use a smaller selection.
Q.191
Which of the following is a component of the skill of blackboard writing?
  1. 1. Speaking very fast
  2. 2. Turning one's back to the class for long periods
  3. 3. Legibility, proper size and alignment of letters
  4. 4. Writing everything in one colour without highlights

Q.192
The 'evaluation approach' to lesson planning, which links instructional objectives, learning experiences and evaluation, was given by whom?
  1. 1. J.F. Herbart
  2. 2. H.C. Morrison
  3. 3. W.H. Kilpatrick
  4. 4. B.S. Bloom
Answer: 4 — Bloom's approach plans each lesson around objectives stated in behavioural terms, matching activities, and evaluation of those objectives.

Q.197
In item analysis, if 40 out of 100 students answer an item correctly, what is the difficulty index (p) of the item?
  1. 1. 0.60
  2. 2. 0.40
  3. 3. 40
  4. 4. 0.04

Q.200

Assertion (A): Open-book examinations are recommended to promote higher-order thinking.
Reason (R): In open-book examinations, questions test the application and analysis of information rather than its recall.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.
