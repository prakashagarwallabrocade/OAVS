History

Q.101
Consider the following statements about why industrialisation began in Britain.
1. Britain had large deposits of coal and iron ore close to each other.
2. Its colonies supplied raw materials and markets for manufactured goods.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. Political stability, capital from trade and a banking system also helped.
Q.102
In NCERT's account, what does 'proto-industrialisation' refer to?
  1. 1. The first factories set up in Manchester
  2. 2. Large-scale production for international markets, done in rural homes before the factory age
  3. 3. Industrialisation in the colonies after 1900
  4. 4. The use of steam engines in agriculture
Answer: 2 — Merchants supplied money to peasants and artisans in the countryside to produce for international markets, before factories appeared.
Q.103
Chartism, a working-class movement in 19th-century Britain, mainly demanded what?
  1. 1. The destruction of machines
  2. 2. Votes for all adult men and other parliamentary reforms
  3. 3. The abolition of the monarchy
  4. 4. Independence for colonies
Answer: 2 — The People's Charter (1838) demanded universal male suffrage, secret ballot, and payment of MPs, among other reforms.
Q.104
Which economist argued, in 'The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money' (1936), that government spending could pull economies out of depression?
  1. 1. Adam Smith
  2. 2. Karl Marx
  3. 3. John Maynard Keynes
  4. 4. Milton Friedman
Answer: 3 — Keynes stressed aggregate demand and public spending. His ideas shaped policy after the Depression and at Bretton Woods.

  4. 4. Narayana Guru

Geography

Q.121
On 21 June, which of the following Indian cities has the longest daytime?
  1. 1. Bhubaneswar
  2. 2. Chennai
  3. 3. Kanyakumari
  4. 4. Srinagar
Answer: 4 — On the summer solstice, day length increases with latitude in the Northern Hemisphere. Srinagar (about 34° N) is the northernmost of these.
Q.122
India's longitudinal extent is about 30° (from about 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E). What is the approximate difference in local time between its eastern and western ends?
  1. 1. 2 hours
  2. 2. 1 hour
  3. 3. 30 minutes
  4. 4. 4 hours
Answer: 1 — 30° × 4 minutes = 120 minutes = 2 hours. This is why a single standard time (82°30′ E) is used.
Q.123
Which of the following is NOT evidence for the continental drift theory?
  1. 1. The jigsaw fit of the coastlines of South America and Africa
  2. 2. The distribution of present-day ocean currents
  3. 3. Glossopteris fossils found in India, Africa, Australia and Antarctica
  4. 4. Tillite deposits of the same age on different continents
Answer: 2 — The jigsaw fit, matching fossils, rocks of the same age and tillite (glacial deposits) support drift. Ocean currents do not.
Q.124
The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is an example of which type of plate boundary?
  1. 1. Convergent
  2. 2. Divergent
  3. 3. Transform
  4. 4. Collision
Answer: 2 — Plates move apart at mid-ocean ridges, where new crust forms (sea-floor spreading).
Q.125
Which earthquake waves travel fastest and are the first to be recorded by a seismograph?
  1. 1. S-waves
  2. 2. Love waves
  3. 3. Rayleigh waves
  4. 4. P-waves
Answer: 4 — Primary (P) waves are longitudinal, travel through solids, liquids and gases, and arrive first.
Q.126
Why are igneous rocks called 'primary rocks'?
  1. 1. Because they are always found on the surface
  2. 2. Because all other rocks are ultimately derived from them
  3. 3. Because they contain fossils
  4. 4. Because they are the softest rocks
Answer: 2 — Igneous rocks form first from magma. Sedimentary and metamorphic rocks are later formed from them.
Q.127
A tributary valley left high above the floor of a main glacial valley, often with a waterfall, is called what?
  1. 1. Cirque
  2. 2. Drumlin
  3. 3. Esker
  4. 4. Hanging valley
Answer: 4 — The main glacier erodes its valley more deeply than the smaller tributary glaciers, leaving 'hanging' valleys.
Q.128
Chilika Lake is separated from the Bay of Bengal by which landform?
  1. 1. A coral reef
  2. 2. A sea cliff
  3. 3. A sand bar / spit
  4. 4. A delta
Answer: 3 — Chilika is a lagoon cut off from the sea by a long sand bar (spit) built by coastal deposition, with narrow mouths linking it to the sea.
Q.129
In which layer of the atmosphere do most meteors burn up?
  1. 1. Troposphere
  2. 2. Stratosphere
  3. 3. Mesosphere
  4. 4. Exosphere
Answer: 3 — The mesosphere (about 50–80 km) is where most meteors burn up. It is also the coldest layer.
Q.130
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The highest temperatures are recorded in subtropical deserts, not at the Equator.
Reason (R): The Equator has heavy cloud cover and rainfall, while subtropical deserts have clear skies.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.
Answer: 1 — Clouds and rain reduce insolation at the Equator. Cloudless desert skies allow intense heating. R explains A.
Q.131
During winter, which jet stream blows over north India and helps bring western disturbances?
  1. 1. Tropical easterly jet stream
  2. 2. Polar jet stream
  3. 3. Somali jet
  4. 4. Sub-tropical westerly jet stream
Answer: 4 — The sub-tropical westerly jet lies south of the Himalayas in winter. The tropical easterly jet appears over the peninsula in summer.
Q.132
In the Northern Hemisphere, how do winds circulate around an anticyclone?
  1. 1. Anticlockwise, blowing inward to a low-pressure centre
  2. 2. Clockwise, blowing outward from a high-pressure centre
  3. 3. Clockwise, blowing inward to a low-pressure centre
  4. 4. Anticlockwise, blowing outward from a high-pressure centre
Answer: 2 — An anticyclone is a high-pressure system. Winds diverge outward and turn clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere (anticlockwise in the Southern).
Q.133
Which climate classification is based on precipitation effectiveness and thermal (temperature) efficiency?
  1. 1. Koeppen's classification
  2. 2. Thornthwaite's classification
  3. 3. Trewartha's classification
  4. 4. Strahler's genetic classification
Answer: 2 — C.W. Thornthwaite's scheme used the water balance through precipitation and evaporation. Koeppen's is based on temperature, precipitation and vegetation.
Q.134
Which part of the ocean floor is the shallow, gently sloping extension of the continent, rich in fish and oil and gas deposits?
  1. 1. Continental shelf
  2. 2. Continental slope
  3. 3. Abyssal plain
  4. 4. Oceanic trench
Answer: 1 — Continental shelves (up to about 200 m deep) support most commercial fisheries and offshore oil fields, such as Mumbai High.
Q.135
On a map, the bearing of place B from place A is 270°. In which direction is B from A?
  1. 1. East
  2. 2. West
  3. 3. South
  4. 4. North
Answer: 2 — Bearings are measured clockwise from north: 0° N, 90° E, 180° S, 270° W.
Q.136
What does a climograph show for a place?
  1. 1. Contours and relief
  2. 2. Monthly temperature and rainfall together

Economics

Q.148
What does 'vulnerability to poverty' describe?
  1. 1. The number of people already below the poverty line
  2. 2. The average income of poor households
  3. 3. The poverty of urban slum dwellers only
  4. 4. The greater probability of certain communities or individuals becoming, or remaining, poor in the coming years
Answer: 4 — Vulnerability is about risk. For example, landless labourers and backward castes are more likely to fall into poverty after a disaster.
Q.149
Hunger that occurs in rural areas during the off-season, when agricultural work is not available, is called what?
  1. 1. Seasonal hunger
  2. 2. Chronic hunger
  3. 3. Hidden hunger
  4. 4. Absolute hunger
Answer: 1 — Seasonal hunger is linked to the cycle of food growing and harvesting. Chronic hunger results from persistently inadequate diets.
Q.150
The Public Distribution System in India has its origins in rationing introduced in which decade, against the backdrop of the Bengal Famine?
  1. 1. 1960s
  2. 2. 1940s
  3. 3. 1920s
  4. 4. 1970s
Answer: 2 — Rationing began in the 1940s. PDS was strengthened in the 1960s, and Targeted PDS came in 1997.
Q.151
What is the Net Attendance Ratio?

  3. 3. The percentage of adults who are literate
  4. 4. The total number of children aged 14–15 attending school as a percentage of all children in that age group

Q.152
NCERT cites the over-use of which resource in Punjab, Haryana and parts of other states as a threat to sustainable development?
  1. 1. Coal
  2. 2. Natural gas
  3. 3. Groundwater
  4. 4. Iron ore

Q.158
According to Keynes, which three motives explain why people hold money?
  1. 1. Saving, investment and consumption
  2. 2. Transaction, precautionary and speculative
  3. 3. Production, distribution and exchange
  4. 4. Income, profit and interest
Answer: 2 — People hold money for daily transactions, for unforeseen needs, and to profit from changes in interest rates or bond prices.
Q.159
What is the difference between the primary and secondary capital markets?
  1. 1. The primary market is for short-term funds, and the secondary market for long-term funds
  2. 2. The primary market is run by the RBI, and the secondary market by banks
  3. 3. The primary market deals with new issues of securities, and the secondary market with trading in existing securities
  4. 4. There is no difference
Answer: 3 — Companies raise fresh capital in the primary market (e.g. IPOs). Already-issued shares change hands in the secondary market (stock exchanges).
Q.160
What is the 'balance of trade' of a country?
  1. 1. The total of all foreign currency reserves
  2. 2. The difference between government income and expenditure
  3. 3. The difference between the value of its exports and imports of goods
  4. 4. Its total foreign debt
Answer: 3 — When imports of goods exceed exports there is a trade deficit. The balance of payments is wider and includes services and capital flows.

  3. 3. Eleven
  4. 4. Fifteen
Answer: 2 — The Drafting Committee (constituted 29 August 1947) had seven members, including Ambedkar.
Q.174
When it was adopted, how many Articles and Schedules did the Constitution of India have?
  1. 1. 448 Articles and 12 Schedules
  2. 2. 395 Articles and 12 Schedules
  3. 3. 395 Articles and 8 Schedules
  4. 4. 370 Articles and 10 Schedules
Answer: 3 — The original Constitution had 395 Articles in 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. Today there are about 448 Articles and 12 Schedules.
Q.175
Which Article abolishes titles (other than military or academic distinctions)?
  1. 1. Article 17
  2. 2. Article 16
  3. 3. Article 18
  4. 4. Article 15
Answer: 3 — Article 18 is part of the Right to Equality (Articles 14–18). Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles in this sense.
Q.176
During a National Emergency, which Fundamental Rights cannot be suspended?
  1. 1. Articles 20 and 21
  2. 2. Articles 14 and 19
  3. 3. Articles 19 and 32
  4. 4. Articles 25 and 26
Answer: 1 — After the 44th Amendment (1978), the rights under Article 20 (protection in respect of conviction) and Article 21 (life and personal liberty) remain enforceable even during an emergency.
Q.177
During a National Emergency, the term of the Lok Sabha can be extended by Parliament by up to how long at a time?
  1. 1. Six months
  2. 2. One year
  3. 3. Two years
  4. 4. Five years
Answer: 2 — Under Article 83(2), it can be extended by one year at a time, and not beyond six months after the emergency ends.
Q.178
Consider the following statements about the election of the Vice-President of India.
1. The electoral college consists of all members (elected and nominated) of both Houses of Parliament.
2. Members of State Legislative Assemblies also vote in it.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 1 — Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is wrong: unlike the presidential election, State MLAs do not vote for the Vice-President.
Q.179
According to NCERT, in what respect is democracy clearly better than other forms of government?
  1. 1. It always produces faster economic growth
  2. 2. It completely removes poverty and inequality
  3. 3. It makes decisions faster than a dictatorship
  4. 4. It produces an accountable, responsive and legitimate government
Answer: 4 — Democracy may be slower, but it is accountable, responsive and legitimate. Its economic outcomes are not always better.
Q.180
To whom does the Speaker of the Lok Sabha address the letter of resignation?
  1. 1. The Deputy Speaker
  2. 2. The President
  3. 3. The Prime Minister
  4. 4. The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
Answer: 1 — Under Article 94, the Speaker resigns by writing to the Deputy Speaker, and the Deputy Speaker by writing to the Speaker.

Teaching Methodology

Q.181
Why is Social Studies described as an 'integrated' subject?
  1. 1. It is taught only through projects
  2. 2. It replaces all other school subjects
  3. 3. It draws content from history, geography, civics, economics and other social sciences around common themes
  4. 4. It is taught only in primary classes
Answer: 3 — Social Studies unifies material from many social sciences to help pupils understand human relationships and society.
Q.182
A student uses the concept of 'rain shadow' to explain why the leeward side of the Western Ghats is dry. Which level of Bloom's taxonomy does this show?
  1. 1. Knowledge
  2. 2. Synthesis
  3. 3. Evaluation
  4. 4. Application
Answer: 4 — Using a learnt concept to explain a new situation is application.
Q.183
Which of the following educationists is associated with a taxonomy of the psychomotor domain?
  1. 1. D.R. Krathwohl
  2. 2. B.S. Bloom (cognitive)
  3. 3. H.C. Morrison
  4. 4. R.H. Dave
Answer: 4 — Psychomotor taxonomies were proposed by R.H. Dave (imitation → naturalisation), E.J. Simpson and A. Harrow. Krathwohl worked on the affective domain.
Q.184
Which of the following is a guiding principle of the National Curriculum Framework 2005?
  1. 1. Connecting knowledge to life outside the school
  2. 2. Strict reliance on textbooks
  3. 3. Rote learning of facts
  4. 4. Making examinations more rigid
Answer: 1 — NCF 2005's principles include connecting knowledge to life outside school, moving away from rote learning, and flexible examinations.
Q.185
In which year was the National Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF-SE), based on NEP 2020, released?
  1. 1. 2020
  2. 2. 2005
  3. 3. 2023
  4. 4. 2021
Answer: 3 — NCF-SE was released in 2023 (the foundational-stage NCF came in 2022).
Q.186
In the unit approach to organising Social Studies content, what is a 'unit'?
  1. 1. A single period of 40 minutes
  2. 2. One page of the textbook
  3. 3. One examination question
  4. 4. A large block of related content organised around a central theme or problem
Answer: 4 — A unit is a meaningful whole (e.g. 'Our Water Resources') taught over several lessons.
Q.187
Which of the following is an example of a formal discussion technique?
  1. 1. Casual classroom conversation
  2. 2. Buzz talk among two neighbours
  3. 3. Debate
  4. 4. Informal question time
Answer: 3 — Formal discussion includes debate, panel discussion, symposium and seminar, which have fixed rules and roles.
Q.188
Consider the following statements.
1. In the problem method, the emphasis is on mental (reflective) activity to solve a problem.
2. In the project method, the emphasis is on a purposeful activity carried out in a natural (real-life) setting.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. The problem method stresses thinking, while the project method stresses doing, often with physical and social activity.
Q.189
Teaching history through the life stories of great people such as Ashoka, Kharavela and Gopabandhu Das is which method, especially suited to lower classes?
  1. 1. Source method
  2. 2. Biographical method
  3. 3. Topical method
  4. 4. Regressive method
Answer: 2 — Children identify with heroes, so biographies create interest in history.
Q.190
A teacher first lets students observe the local market (experience) and then leads them to the economic concept of demand and supply (reasoning). Which maxim is being followed?
  1. 1. From empirical to rational
  2. 2. From whole to part
  3. 3. From abstract to concrete
  4. 4. From complex to simple
Answer: 1 — Empirical means based on observation and experience, and rational means based on reasoning. Learning moves from the first to the second.
Q.191
Students act as the Speaker, ministers and opposition members to show how a Bill is passed. This is an example of what?
  1. 1. Role play (simulation)
  2. 2. Lecture
  3. 3. Supervised study
  4. 4. Assignment
Answer: 1 — Role play and simulation let learners experience processes such as parliamentary procedure directly.
Q.192
When a student gives an incomplete answer, the teacher asks further questions to lead them to the correct and complete answer. Which skill is this?
  1. 1. Skill of reinforcement
  2. 2. Skill of closure
  3. 3. Skill of stimulus variation
  4. 4. Skill of probing questions
Answer: 4 — Probing includes prompting, seeking further information, refocusing and redirection.
Q.193
Which of the following is a desirable component of the skill of explaining?
  1. 1. Using beginning and concluding statements and explaining links
  2. 2. Using irrelevant statements
  3. 3. Lack of continuity
  4. 4. Using vague words
Answer: 1 — Desirable components are clear beginning and concluding statements, explaining links and fluency. Irrelevance and vagueness are undesirable.
Q.194
In a typical Social Studies lesson plan, what is the purpose of 'testing previous knowledge'?
  1. 1. To give final marks
  2. 2. To link the new lesson to what the students already know
  3. 3. To end the lesson
  4. 4. To assign homework
Answer: 2 — Testing previous knowledge, part of preparation or introduction, connects new learning with existing ideas.
Q.195
What is the main use of a workbook in Social Studies?
  1. 1. To replace the textbook entirely
  2. 2. To record attendance
  3. 3. To give students exercises and activities that reinforce what they learn from the textbook
  4. 4. To display maps in the classroom
Answer: 3 — Workbooks provide map work, fill-in exercises and activities that encourage self-study and practice.
Q.196
In Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience, which of the following is the most abstract?
  1. 1. Contrived experiences
  2. 2. Dramatised experiences
  3. 3. Field trips
  4. 4. Verbal symbols
Answer: 4 — The cone moves from direct, purposeful experience at the base to verbal symbols at the top (most abstract).
Q.197
A question beginning 'Why did …' or 'Explain how …' mainly tests which objective?
  1. 1. Knowledge (recall)
  2. 2. Skill
  3. 3. Understanding
  4. 4. Attitude
Answer: 3 — Explaining reasons and relationships needs understanding, not just recall of facts.
Q.198
A test is said to be objective when which of the following is true?
  1. 1. It has only essay questions
  2. 2. It is very long
  3. 3. Different examiners give the same score to the same answer
  4. 4. It is set by the head of the school
Answer: 3 — Objectivity means that the examiner's personal judgement does not affect scoring, as with well-made MCQs.
Q.199
A purposeful collection of a student's work over time (projects, maps, write-ups and reflections), used for assessment, is called what?
  1. 1. Anecdotal record
  2. 2. Portfolio
  3. 3. Checklist
  4. 4. Achievement test
Answer: 2 — Portfolios show progress over time and support continuous and comprehensive evaluation.
Q.200
In assessing a Social Studies project, what is a rubric?
  1. 1. A list of students' names
  2. 2. A type of objective question
  3. 3. A scoring guide that lists criteria and describes different levels of performance
  4. 4. A map symbol
Answer: 3 — Rubrics make the grading of projects and presentations transparent and consistent.
