Q.1
The following sentence has been split into four segments. Identify the segment that contains a grammatical error.
Each of the students / have submitted / their project / on time.
  1. 1. have submitted
  2. 2. Each of the students
  3. 3. their project
  4. 4. on time
Answer: 1 — 'Each of' takes a singular verb: 'Each of the students has submitted…'.
Q.2
The following sentence has been split into four segments. Identify the segment that contains a grammatical error.
Neither the principal / nor the teachers was / aware of the / change in the schedule.
  1. 1. Neither the principal
  2. 2. aware of the
  3. 3. nor the teachers was
  4. 4. change in the schedule
Answer: 3 — With 'neither…nor', the verb agrees with the nearer subject. 'Teachers' is plural, so it should be 'nor the teachers were'.
Q.3
The following sentence has been split into four segments. Identify the segment that contains a grammatical error.
He is senior than me / in service, / but I am / more experienced.
  1. 1. in service,
  2. 2. but I am
  3. 3. more experienced.
  4. 4. He is senior than me
Answer: 4 — Latin comparatives such as senior, junior, superior and inferior take 'to', not 'than': 'He is senior to me'.
Q.4
Select the most appropriate option to fill in the blank.
The committee has not yet arrived ______ a final decision.
  1. 1. in
  2. 2. to
  3. 3. at
  4. 4. for
Answer: 3 — The fixed expression is 'arrive at a decision'.
Q.5
Select the most appropriate option to fill in the blank.
Hardly had the teacher entered the classroom ______ the students stood up.
  1. 1. than
  2. 2. then
  3. 3. when
  4. 4. as
Answer: 3 — 'Hardly' and 'scarcely' are followed by 'when' (or 'before'). 'No sooner' takes 'than'.
Q.6
Select the most appropriate option to fill in the blank.
If I ______ you, I would accept the offer without delay.
  1. 1. am
  2. 2. were
  3. 3. had been
  4. 4. will be
Answer: 2 — An imaginary present condition uses the subjunctive 'were' for every subject: 'If I were you…'.
Q.7
Select the most appropriate synonym of the given word.
Benevolent
  1. 1. Malicious
  2. 2. Hesitant
  3. 3. Arrogant
  4. 4. Kind-hearted
Answer: 4 — Benevolent means well-meaning and kindly, i.e. kind-hearted. 'Malicious' is its opposite.
Q.8
Select the most appropriate synonym of the given word.
Ephemeral
  1. 1. Eternal
  2. 2. Short-lived
  3. 3. Ancient
  4. 4. Visible
Answer: 2 — Ephemeral means lasting for a very short time. 'Eternal' is the opposite.
Q.9
Select the most appropriate antonym of the given word.
Frugal
  1. 1. Thrifty
  2. 2. Extravagant
  3. 3. Careful
  4. 4. Economical
Answer: 2 — Frugal means sparing or economical with money. Its opposite is extravagant. 'Thrifty' and 'economical' are synonyms.
Q.10
Select the most appropriate antonym of the given word.
Obscure
  1. 1. Vague
  2. 2. Hidden
  3. 3. Prominent
  4. 4. Ambiguous
Answer: 3 — Obscure means not well known or not clearly seen. 'Prominent' (well known, conspicuous) is the opposite. The other options are near-synonyms.
Q.11
Select the correctly spelt word.
  1. 1. Acommodation
  2. 2. Accommodation
  3. 3. Accomodation
  4. 4. Acomodation
Answer: 2 — Accommodation has a double 'c' and a double 'm'.
Q.12
Identify the misspelt word in the given sentence.
The goverment announced new measures to curb inflation.
  1. 1. announced
  2. 2. measures
  3. 3. goverment
  4. 4. inflation
Answer: 3 — The correct spelling is 'government' (government).
Q.13
Select the most appropriate meaning of the given idiom.
To burn the midnight oil
  1. 1. To waste resources carelessly
  2. 2. To start a quarrel
  3. 3. To light a lamp for prayer
  4. 4. To work or study late into the night
Answer: 4 — To 'burn the midnight oil' is to stay up late working or studying.
Q.14
Select the most appropriate meaning of the given idiom.
A bolt from the blue
  1. 1. A violent thunderstorm
  2. 2. A sudden and unexpected event
  3. 3. A long-awaited reward
  4. 4. A clear and simple answer
Answer: 2 — A bolt from the blue is a complete surprise, like lightning from a clear sky.
Q.15
Select the option that can be used as a one-word substitute for the given group of words.
One who is present everywhere
  1. 1. Omniscient
  2. 2. Omnipotent
  3. 3. Omnivorous
  4. 4. Omnipresent
Answer: 4 — Omnipresent = present everywhere. Omniscient = all-knowing, omnipotent = all-powerful, omnivorous = eating all kinds of food.
Q.16
Select the option that can be used as a one-word substitute for the given group of words.
Government by a small group of people
  1. 1. Oligarchy
  2. 2. Autocracy
  3. 3. Monarchy
  4. 4. Anarchy
Answer: 1 — Oligarchy is rule by a few. Autocracy is rule by one person with absolute power, monarchy is rule by a king or queen, and anarchy is the absence of government.
Q.17
Select the most appropriate option to improve the underlined part of the sentence. If no improvement is needed, select 'No improvement'.
He has been living in Bhubaneswar since five years.
  1. 1. for five years
  2. 2. from five years
  3. 3. since five years ago
  4. 4. No improvement
Answer: 1 — 'For' is used with a period of time (five years). 'Since' is used with a point of time (since 2021).
Q.18
Select the option that expresses the given sentence in passive voice.
The teacher was teaching the students.
  1. 1. The students were taught by the teacher.
  2. 2. The students had been taught by the teacher.
  3. 3. The students are being taught by the teacher.
  4. 4. The students were being taught by the teacher.
Answer: 4 — Past continuous active (was teaching) becomes 'were/was being + past participle' in the passive.
Q.19
Select the option that expresses the given sentence in indirect speech.
He said to me, "Where are you going?"
  1. 1. He asked me where was I going.
  2. 2. He said to me where I am going.
  3. 3. He asked me that where I was going.
  4. 4. He asked me where I was going.
Answer: 4 — In reported questions, 'said to' becomes 'asked', the question takes statement word order (I was going), the tense moves back, and 'that' is not used.
Q.20
The given sentence is divided into parts. The first part is fixed. Arrange the remaining parts in the correct order.
The Konark Sun Temple,
P: is a masterpiece
Q: built in the 13th century
R: of Kalinga architecture
S: by King Narasimhadeva I,
  1. 1. SQPR
  2. 2. QSPR
  3. 3. PRQS
  4. 4. QPSR
Answer: 2 — The Konark Sun Temple, built in the 13th century (Q) by King Narasimhadeva I (S), is a masterpiece (P) of Kalinga architecture (R).
