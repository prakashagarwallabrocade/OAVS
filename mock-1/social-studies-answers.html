History

Q.101
The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, the immediate cause of the First World War, took place in which city?
  1. 1. Vienna
  2. 2. Sarajevo
  3. 3. Belgrade
  4. 4. Berlin
Answer: 2 — Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary was shot at Sarajevo (Bosnia) on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip.
Q.102
Who invented the Spinning Jenny (1764), which allowed one worker to spin several threads at once?
  1. 1. Richard Arkwright
  2. 2. Samuel Crompton
  3. 3. James Hargreaves
  4. 4. Edmund Cartwright
Answer: 3 — James Hargreaves invented the Spinning Jenny. Arkwright invented the water frame, Crompton the spinning mule and Cartwright the power loom.
Q.103
The Great Depression began with the crash of the Wall Street stock exchange in which year?
  1. 1. 1919
  2. 2. 1933
  3. 3. 1939
  4. 4. 1929
Answer: 4 — The Wall Street crash of October 1929 started the Great Depression, which lasted through the 1930s.

Geography

Q.121
The local time changes by how much for every 1° of longitude?
  1. 1. 1 minute
  2. 2. 15 minutes
  3. 3. 1 hour
  4. 4. 4 minutes
Answer: 4 — The Earth turns 360° in 24 hours (1,440 minutes), so 1440 ÷ 360 = 4 minutes per degree.
Q.122
When it is 12 noon at Greenwich (0°), what will be the local time at a place on 90° E longitude?
  1. 1. 6:00 a.m.
  2. 2. 3:00 p.m.
  3. 3. 6:00 p.m.
  4. 4. 9:00 p.m.
Answer: 3 — 90° × 4 minutes = 360 minutes = 6 hours. Places east of Greenwich are ahead, so 12 noon + 6 hours = 6 p.m.
Q.123
On which date are the Sun's rays vertical over the Tropic of Capricorn?
  1. 1. 21 June
  2. 2. 22 December
  3. 3. 21 March
  4. 4. 23 September
Answer: 2 — 22 December is the winter solstice for the Northern Hemisphere, when the Sun is overhead at 23½° S. On 21 June it is over the Tropic of Cancer, and on 21 March and 23 September (the equinoxes) it is over the Equator.
Q.124
According to Alfred Wegener's Continental Drift Theory, what was the name of the vast ocean that surrounded the super-continent Pangaea?
  1. 1. Tethys
  2. 2. Laurasia
  3. 3. Gondwana
  4. 4. Panthalassa
Answer: 4 — Pangaea was surrounded by Panthalassa. The Tethys Sea separated Laurasia (north) from Gondwanaland (south).
Q.125
Which discontinuity separates the Earth's crust from the mantle?
  1. 1. Gutenberg discontinuity
  2. 2. Lehmann discontinuity
  3. 3. Conrad discontinuity
  4. 4. Mohorovičić (Moho) discontinuity
Answer: 4 — The Moho separates the crust and mantle. The Gutenberg discontinuity lies between the mantle and core, the Lehmann between the outer and inner core, and the Conrad between the upper and lower crust.
Q.126
Marble is a metamorphic rock formed from which rock?
  1. 1. Sandstone
  2. 2. Shale
  3. 3. Limestone
  4. 4. Granite
Answer: 3 — Limestone becomes marble, sandstone becomes quartzite, shale becomes slate and granite becomes gneiss.
Q.127
Stalactites and stalagmites are landforms created mainly by which agent?
  1. 1. Wind in deserts
  2. 2. Groundwater in limestone regions
  3. 3. Glaciers in mountains
  4. 4. Sea waves along coasts
Answer: 2 — Groundwater dissolves and re-deposits calcium carbonate in limestone (karst) areas, forming stalactites (hanging from the cave roof) and stalagmites (rising from the floor).
Q.128
Which layer of the atmosphere contains most of the ozone?
  1. 1. Troposphere
  2. 2. Mesosphere
  3. 3. Thermosphere
  4. 4. Stratosphere
Answer: 4 — The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere (about 15–35 km up) and absorbs ultraviolet radiation.
Q.129
The 'Roaring Forties' are associated with which planetary winds?
  1. 1. Trade winds
  2. 2. Westerlies
  3. 3. Polar easterlies
  4. 4. Monsoon winds
Answer: 2 — In the Southern Hemisphere, the westerlies blow with great force over the open ocean around 40° S, so these latitudes are called the Roaring Forties.

Q.132
On which days do spring tides occur?
  1. 1. First and last quarter moon
  2. 2. Only on full moon
  3. 3. Only during equinoxes
  4. 4. New moon and full moon
Answer: 4 — On new moon and full moon days the Sun, Moon and Earth are in a straight line, and their combined pull gives the highest tides (spring tides). Neap tides occur at the quarter moons.
Q.133
On a topographical map, contour lines drawn very close together indicate what?
  1. 1. A gentle slope
  2. 2. A flat plain
  3. 3. A water body
  4. 4. A steep slope
Answer: 4 — Closely spaced contours mean height changes quickly over a short distance, i.e. a steep slope. Widely spaced contours show a gentle slope.
Q.134
Lines on a map joining places that receive equal rainfall are called what?
  1. 1. Isotherms
  2. 2. Isohyets
  3. 3. Isobars
  4. 4. Isohalines
Answer: 2 — Isohyets join places of equal rainfall, isotherms equal temperature, isobars equal pressure and isohalines equal salinity.
Q.135
The '10 per cent law' of energy transfer between trophic levels in an ecosystem was given by whom?
  1. 1. Arthur Tansley
  2. 2. Ernst Haeckel
  3. 3. Raymond Lindeman
  4. 4. Charles Darwin
Answer: 3 — Lindeman (1942) showed that only about 10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next. Tansley coined the term 'ecosystem' and Haeckel coined 'ecology'.

Q.145
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Tsunami waves may be barely noticeable in the deep open ocean but become very high near the coast.
Reason (R): As the waves enter shallow water their speed decreases and their height increases.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.

Economics

Q.153

Q.154

  1. 1. Shortage of goods
  2. 2. Inflation
  3. 3. Lack of banks
  4. 4. Double coincidence of wants
Answer: 4 — In barter, each party must want what the other has (double coincidence of wants). Money acts as a medium of exchange and removes this need.

Q.160

Civics / Polity

Q.161
In Belgium, the 'community government' is elected by whom?
  1. 1. People belonging to one language community
  2. 2. People of one particular region only
  3. 3. Members of the central Parliament
  4. 4. People living in Brussels only
Answer: 1 — The community government is elected by people of one language community (Dutch, French or German), wherever they live. It handles cultural, educational and language matters.
Q.162
In Sri Lanka, the Act of 1956 recognised which language as the only official language?
  1. 1. Tamil
  2. 2. English
  3. 3. Sinhala
  4. 4. Both Sinhala and Tamil
Answer: 3 — The 1956 Act made Sinhala the only official language, ignoring Tamil. This majoritarian measure increased tension with Sri Lankan Tamils.
Q.163
Education, forests, trade unions, marriage and adoption are subjects in which list?
  1. 1. Union List
  2. 2. Concurrent List
  3. 3. State List
  4. 4. Residuary subjects
Answer: 2 — These are in the Concurrent List, on which both the Union and the States can make laws. Education and forests were moved there by the 42nd Amendment (1976).
Q.164
In India, the power to make laws on 'residuary subjects' (not in any of the three lists) belongs to which body?
  1. 1. State Legislatures
  2. 2. Both Union and States
  3. 3. Supreme Court
  4. 4. Union Parliament
Answer: 4 — Under Article 248, residuary powers lie with the Union Parliament. Subjects like computer software are examples.
Q.165

  1. 1. 74th Amendment, 1992
  2. 2. 73rd Amendment, 1992
  3. 3. 42nd Amendment, 1976
  4. 4. 61st Amendment, 1988
Answer: 2 — The 73rd Amendment covers rural local bodies (Panchayats). The 74th covers urban local bodies (Municipalities).
Q.166
Which body conducts elections to Panchayats and Municipalities in a state?

The anti-defection law is contained in which Schedule of the Constitution?

Bolivia's 'water war' of 2000 in the city of Cochabamba was a popular struggle against what?
  1. 1. Construction of a large dam

Answer: 2 — Protests forced the government to cancel the contract that had sold Cochabamba's water rights to a multinational company, which had raised water prices four-fold.
Q.171
Consider the following statements about the movement for democracy in Nepal.
1. In April 2006, a mass movement forced King Gyanendra to restore Parliament.
2. The movement was led by the Seven Party Alliance (SPA) with support from the Maoists.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. The SPA, supported by the Maoists, led the April 2006 movement, and the King restored the House of Representatives. Nepal later abolished the monarchy (2008).
Q.172
On which date did the Constituent Assembly adopt the Constitution of India?
  1. 1. 26 November 1949
  2. 2. 26 January 1950
  3. 3. 15 August 1947
  4. 4. 9 December 1946
Answer: 1 — The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now Constitution Day) and came into force on 26 January 1950. The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946.
Q.173
The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by which Amendment?
  1. 1. 44th Amendment, 1978
  2. 2. 24th Amendment, 1971
  3. 3. 52nd Amendment, 1985
  4. 4. 42nd Amendment, 1976
Answer: 4 — The 42nd Amendment added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble.
Q.174
Which Article did Dr B.R. Ambedkar call the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution?
  1. 1. Article 14
  2. 2. Article 19
  3. 3. Article 21
  4. 4. Article 32
Answer: 4 — Article 32, the Right to Constitutional Remedies, lets citizens move the Supreme Court directly to enforce Fundamental Rights.
Q.175
The Right to free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years is provided under which Article?
  1. 1. Article 21A
  2. 2. Article 45
  3. 3. Article 51A
  4. 4. Article 29
Answer: 1 — Article 21A was inserted by the 86th Amendment (2002). Article 45 now deals with early childhood care for children below 6 years.
Q.176
What is the maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha as provided in the Constitution?
  1. 1. 245
  2. 2. 238
  3. 3. 552
  4. 4. 250
Answer: 4 — The Rajya Sabha can have up to 250 members: 238 representatives of States and UTs plus 12 nominated by the President. Its present strength is 245.
Q.177
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
Reason (R): The Rajya Sabha can delay a Money Bill only for 14 days.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.
Answer: 2 — Both statements are true (Articles 109 and 110). However, a Money Bill is introduced only in the Lok Sabha because the popularly elected House controls finance, not because of the 14-day limit.
Q.178
Up to what age does a judge of the Supreme Court of India hold office?
  1. 1. 62 years
  2. 2. 65 years
  3. 3. 60 years
  4. 4. 70 years
Answer: 2 — Supreme Court judges hold office until 65 years of age. High Court judges retire at 62.
Q.179
Who among the following is NOT part of the electoral college for the election of the President of India?
  1. 1. Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha
  2. 2. Elected members of the Lok Sabha
  3. 3. Elected members of the Rajya Sabha
  4. 4. Elected members of the Legislative Assembly of Delhi
Answer: 1 — The electoral college has only elected members: of both Houses of Parliament and of the Legislative Assemblies of the States, Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated members do not vote.
Q.180
The challenge of making the transition to democracy and then establishing a democratic government is called what?
  1. 1. Foundational challenge
  2. 2. Challenge of expansion
  3. 3. Challenge of deepening democracy
  4. 4. Challenge of legitimacy
Answer: 1 — NCERT identifies three challenges: foundational (moving to democracy), expansion (applying democratic principles to all regions, groups and institutions) and deepening (strengthening democratic institutions).

Teaching Methodology

Q.181
Who defined the social studies as 'the social sciences simplified for pedagogical purposes'?
  1. 1. John Michaelis
  2. 2. Edgar Bruce Wesley
  3. 3. W.H. Kilpatrick
  4. 4. John Dewey
Answer: 2 — E.B. Wesley's definition stresses that social studies are content selected and simplified from the social sciences for school teaching.
Q.182
Consider the following statements.
1. Social Studies is an integrated area of study meant mainly for school-level instruction.
2. Social sciences are separate disciplines (such as economics and political science) concerned with research and advanced study.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  1. 1. Only 1
  2. 2. Only 2
  3. 3. Both 1 and 2
  4. 4. Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: 3 — Both are correct. Social Studies integrates content from history, geography, civics and economics for teaching, while the social sciences are specialised academic disciplines.
Q.183
In Bloom's original taxonomy (1956), which is the highest level of the cognitive domain?
  1. 1. Evaluation
  2. 2. Synthesis
  3. 3. Analysis
  4. 4. Application
Answer: 1 — The order is Knowledge → Comprehension → Application → Analysis → Synthesis → Evaluation. (In the 2001 revision, 'Creating' is the highest.)
Q.184
The taxonomy of objectives in the affective domain (receiving, responding, valuing, organisation, characterisation) was developed mainly by whom?
  1. 1. E.J. Simpson
  2. 2. David R. Krathwohl
  3. 3. R.H. Dave
  4. 4. Robert Gagné
Answer: 2 — Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia (1964) gave the affective domain taxonomy. Simpson and Dave are known for psychomotor domain taxonomies.
Q.185
Which of the following is an instructional objective written in behavioural (measurable) terms?
  1. 1. The student will understand the geography of Odisha.
  2. 2. The student will appreciate the beauty of Chilika Lake.
  3. 3. The student will know about the lakes of India.
  4. 4. The student will be able to locate Chilika Lake on an outline map of Odisha.
Answer: 4 — 'Locate' is an observable, measurable action. 'Understand', 'appreciate' and 'know' cannot be directly observed, so they are not behavioural terms.
Q.186
In which approach is the same topic taught in successive classes, each time in greater depth and detail?
  1. 1. Chronological approach
  2. 2. Topical approach
  3. 3. Unit approach
  4. 4. Concentric (spiral) approach
Answer: 4 — In the concentric approach, content widens like concentric circles. The topic is introduced simply in lower classes and revisited in more depth later.
Q.187
Who developed the Project Method of teaching?
  1. 1. J.F. Herbart
  2. 2. W.H. Kilpatrick
  3. 3. H.C. Morrison
  4. 4. Friedrich Froebel
Answer: 2 — Kilpatrick, a follower of John Dewey, developed the project method (1918), defining a project as 'a wholehearted purposeful activity proceeding in a social environment'.
Q.188
To develop historical sense, a teacher asks students to examine inscriptions, coins and old documents. Which method is being used?
  1. 1. Lecture method
  2. 2. Dramatisation method
  3. 3. Source method
  4. 4. Socialised recitation
Answer: 3 — The source method uses primary sources (inscriptions, coins, records, letters) so that students build knowledge of the past from evidence.
Q.189
A teacher presents several examples and leads students to arrive at a general rule from them. Which method is this?
  1. 1. Deductive method
  2. 2. Lecture method
  3. 3. Inductive method
  4. 4. Assignment method
Answer: 3 — The inductive method moves from particular examples to a generalisation. The deductive method starts from the rule and applies it to examples.
Q.190
A teacher begins a lesson on Parliament by first discussing the students' own Gram Panchayat. Which maxim of teaching is being followed?
  1. 1. From known to unknown
  2. 2. From analysis to synthesis
  3. 3. From empirical to rational
  4. 4. From whole to part
Answer: 1 — Students already know their local panchayat. The teacher starts from what is familiar and moves to the less familiar national institution.
Q.191
In which technique does the class discuss a topic as a group, with students rather than the teacher leading the discussion?
  1. 1. Socialised recitation
  2. 2. Supervised study
  3. 3. Lecture-cum-demonstration
  4. 4. Programmed instruction
Answer: 1 — In socialised recitation the class works as a social group: students ask questions, discuss and evaluate, and the teacher only guides.
Q.192
Micro-teaching as a technique of teacher training was first developed at which institution?
  1. 1. University of London
  2. 2. University of Chicago
  3. 3. Stanford University
  4. 4. Columbia University
Answer: 3 — Micro-teaching was developed at Stanford University (USA) in 1963 by Dwight W. Allen and colleagues.
Q.193
In Herbart's formal steps of lesson planning, which step comes first?
  1. 1. Presentation
  2. 2. Preparation
  3. 3. Generalisation
  4. 4. Application
Answer: 2 — The Herbartian steps are Preparation → Presentation → Comparison/Association → Generalisation → Application (Recapitulation is often added).
Q.194
The unit plan approach to teaching is mainly associated with which educationist?
  1. 1. J.F. Herbart
  2. 2. H.C. Morrison
  3. 3. B.S. Bloom
  4. 4. John Dewey
Answer: 2 — H.C. Morrison developed the unit plan, with the cycle: exploration, presentation, assimilation, organisation and recitation.
Q.195
Which teaching aid shows the true shape, size and direction of continents and oceans without distortion?
  1. 1. Globe
  2. 2. Wall map
  3. 3. Atlas
  4. 4. Chart
Answer: 1 — Every flat map distorts shape, area or direction to some extent. Only a globe, being a model of the spherical Earth, shows them all correctly.
Q.196
According to Edgar Dale's 'Cone of Experience', which type of experience is the most concrete?
  1. 1. Direct, purposeful experiences
  2. 2. Verbal symbols
  3. 3. Visual symbols
  4. 4. Motion pictures
Answer: 1 — Direct, purposeful experiences form the base of the cone (most concrete), and verbal symbols are at the top (most abstract).
Q.197
Evaluation carried out during the teaching–learning process to improve learning is called what?
  1. 1. Summative evaluation
  2. 2. Placement evaluation
  3. 3. Terminal evaluation
  4. 4. Formative evaluation
Answer: 4 — Formative evaluation is ongoing and gives feedback for improvement. Summative evaluation comes at the end of a course or term.
Q.198
A test designed to identify the specific learning difficulties of students, followed by remedial teaching, is called what?
  1. 1. Diagnostic test
  2. 2. Achievement test
  3. 3. Aptitude test
  4. 4. Intelligence test
Answer: 1 — A diagnostic test locates weak areas and causes of errors, and remedial teaching follows. An achievement test measures how much has been learnt.
Q.199
The blueprint of an achievement test shows the weightage given to which of the following?
  1. 1. Students' previous marks
  2. 2. Number of teachers and classrooms
  3. 3. Objectives, content units and forms of questions
  4. 4. Time-table of the examination
Answer: 3 — A blueprint is a three-dimensional chart giving the weightage to instructional objectives, content and types of questions, so the test is balanced and valid.
Q.200
Read the statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) assesses both scholastic and co-scholastic areas of a learner's development.
Reason (R): CCE depends only on the final term-end written examination.
  1. 1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  2. 2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  3. 3. A is true, but R is false.
  4. 4. A is false, but R is true.
Answer: 3 — A is true: 'comprehensive' covers scholastic and co-scholastic areas. R is false: 'continuous' means evaluation goes on throughout the year, not just in a final exam.
